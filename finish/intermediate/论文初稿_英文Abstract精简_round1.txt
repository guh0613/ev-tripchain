After EV charging load is connected to a distribution network, its effect is not limited to temporal variation; it also shifts spatially with vehicle travel. A fixed load template is therefore insufficient for describing its influence on hosting capacity. Following the line of trip-chain load characterization, probabilistic hosting capacity assessment, and charging navigation analysis, this thesis studies distribution network hosting capacity with stochastic EV charging load. A multi-day continuous trip-chain simulation is used to describe travel, parking, and charging processes and to obtain bus-level time-series charging load. The resulting load is then evaluated under voltage, line, and transformer constraints together with voltage deviation and network-loss indices. Monte Carlo assessment is used as the main method, with deterministic evaluation and voltage sensitivity analysis kept for comparison. A multi-factor charging navigation strategy is further introduced to examine how spatial redistribution of charging demand affects hosting capacity. The case study indicates that navigation can improve EV accommodation without adding distribution equipment, while deterministic mean-template assessment may understate stochastic operating risk. Voltage violation remains the main limiting factor. The results provide a reference for distribution network planning and charging facility siting.
